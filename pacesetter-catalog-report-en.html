Summary

Three disconnects, all measurable

The store and the supplier have run independently for years. The result is not one broken thing: it is three separate problems, each with money attached.

  • Products that no longer exist. 174 of the 222 published products point at codes PaceSetter no longer sells.
  • Invisible stock. There is real inventory at the supplier that the store shows as sold out, or does not show at all.
  • Prices adrift. They were set by hand, product by product. Today 85 sit below the recommended price and one sells below its own cost.

Problem 1

The published catalog no longer matches the supplier's

Of the 1,803 products carrying a PaceSetter code in the database, only 554 have all of their codes still live in the supplier's current catalog. Another 40 have some, and 1,140 have none at all.

Seen from the store it is starker still: of the 222 published products, 174 do not have a single live code. These are pages a customer can buy from, and every such order then has to be resolved by hand, because the supplier no longer ships that code.

And the other way round: PaceSetter sells 1,134 products the store does not offer. Available catalog, with stock and pricing, currently earning nothing.

The cause is identifiable: supplier codes were stored as free text typed by hand (CM700-05, CD561B, CD561C, G0713A (sm)). A large share of them are not dead, merely mis-recorded: 962 codes are a live product with a colour suffix attached. Normalising those recovers roughly 499 more products.

Problem 3

Pricing follows no policy

PaceSetter publishes two prices per product: the distributor cost and the recommended retail price. The gap between them is constant — an exact 40% discount, verified across 29 of 30 sampled products without a single exception.

Comparing the 145 products that are both live at the supplier and published in the store, current margins run from −47% to +366%, with a median of +100%. That is not a pricing policy: it is 145 separate decisions made at different times.

This product loses money on every order, today, and will keep doing so until someone reviews it by hand. With pricing connected to the supplier, a case like this becomes impossible by construction.

An important qualification: selling below the recommended price is not necessarily a mistake — it can be a deliberate commercial decision. What this report demonstrates is not that those 85 prices are wrong, but that nobody can tell which ones are: there is no way to distinguish an intentional discount from a price forgotten three years ago.

Problem 4

Published products the supplier no longer carries

CD1017 Water Drop Award

6 supplier codes · none of them exist at PaceSetter today

The page is still published and buyable, priced between $125.69 and $147.69. Its six codes (CD1017ABL, CD1017ARD, CD1017BBL, CD1017BRD, CD1017CBL, CD1017CRD) do not appear in the supplier's current catalog. It is one of 174 products in that position.

Every order for one of these turns into manual work: find an equivalent, contact the customer, or cancel.

Solution

What synchronisation changes

The system being built connects the store directly to PaceSetter's services (PromoStandards), the same standard the supplier uses to publish catalog, stock and pricing.

TodayWith synchronisation
Stock updated by hand, or not at allRefreshed automatically from the supplier
Prices set one by one, with no policySupplier RRP published; selling below cost becomes impossible
Discontinued products stay on saleDetected and flagged
1,134 available products unpublishedCan be published with complete data
Nobody knows what is out of syncEvery product shows its status

The system is already built and running against PaceSetter's live services: it reads catalog, stock, pricing, images and decoration areas, and pushes to Shopify only what has actually changed.

One piece of groundwork remains and cannot be skipped: reconciling the historical codes in the database against the supplier's. That cross-check is prepared and awaiting review, and it determines how many of the 1,803 products end up connected.

Sources and method. Store database (SQL Server, 8,734 live products, 1,803 carrying a PaceSetter code); PaceSetter's production PromoStandards services (Product Data 1.0.0, Inventory 1.2.1, Pricing & Configuration 1.0.0); the production Shopify store via the Admin API. Data captured on August 20, 2026.

Cost and recommended prices come from the same supplier call requested with both price types (Net and List). The margin comparison covers the 145 products that are simultaneously live at PaceSetter and published in the store; the rest are not comparable because one of the two halves is missing. The $3,612 figure is the sum of per-unit gaps against the recommended price, not an estimate of lost revenue.
